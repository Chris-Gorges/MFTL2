`src/topology/` — Half-edge connectivity {#mftl_mod_topology}
========================================

**Public header:** `include/mftl_topology.h` · **Layer:** 2 ·
**Module group:** @ref mftl_topology

The *navigation* lives in the header as `static inline` functions, because it is
one or two arithmetic operations and a load. What is left for `topology.c` is the
three operations that touch the whole mesh:

| Function | Job |
|---|---|
| `MFTLRebuildTwins` | pair every half-edge with its opposite, from the vertex indices alone |
| `MFTLMarkMeshBoundary` | record which half-edges and vertices lie on the surface's own boundary |
| `MFTLCheckMeshIntegrity` | validate the structure |

---

## The half-edge data structure {#topo_halfedge}

An oriented manifold triangle mesh is stored as a set of *half-edges*: each
undirected edge is split into two oppositely directed halves, one belonging to
each incident triangle.

The classical structure stores four things per half-edge:

$$
  \mathrm{target}(h), \quad \mathrm{twin}(h), \quad \mathrm{next}(h), \quad \mathrm{face}(h)
$$

MFTL stores **two**.

---

## Implicit indexing {#topo_implicit}

Half-edge $3t+k$ **is** corner $k$ of triangle $t$. That is an *invariant*, not a
convention that happens to hold after generation: anything creating a triangle
creates its three half-edges at $3t \ldots 3t+2$, and the pools are laid out so
that no other arrangement is representable.

Therefore

$$
  \mathrm{face}(h) = \left\lfloor h/3 \right\rfloor, \qquad
  \mathrm{next}(h) = 3\left\lfloor h/3 \right\rfloor + (h+1) \bmod 3, \qquad
  \mathrm{prev}(h) = 3\left\lfloor h/3 \right\rfloor + (h+2) \bmod 3
$$

and the origin follows from the target of the previous half-edge:

$$
  \mathrm{origin}(h) = \mathrm{target}(\mathrm{prev}(h))
$$

**One arithmetic op and one load**, where a stored `next` needs two dependent
loads.

### Three consequences

1. A whole family of corruptions — "the next-cycle is broken", "the half-edge
   does not point back at its triangle" — **cannot occur**. That is why
   `MFTLCheckMeshIntegrity` is a third of the size it used to be.
2. The half-edge array needs no capacity of its own: it is $3\times$ the triangle
   capacity, in the same allocation.
3. The price is that the mesh is triangles-only. It already was, everywhere.

@warning `MFTLGetTriangleVertices` returns slot $k$ as the **origin** of
         half-edge $3t+k$, which is the convention `tris.verts` follows and the
         one `tri_write` (in `src/remeshing/`) writes. The three must agree, or
         code that mixes them **silently rotates its triangles**.

## Rebuilding twins {#topo_twins}

`MFTLRebuildTwins` reconstructs every `twin_idx` from the vertex indices alone.
Two half-edges are twins when one runs $u \to v$ and the other $v \to u$.

The pairing is done by bucketing on the **smaller endpoint index**: each
half-edge is pushed onto the chain of $\min(u, v)$, and a second pass over each
chain finds the partner with the opposite direction. That is $O(H)$ with one
random-access stream, against $O(H \log H)$ for a sort of $(\min, \max)$ pairs.

@warning It re-pairs by vertex **slot**. Two bodies that share slot numbering —
         which is what happens if a foreign body's triangles are appended to a
         mesh's own pools — get **welded together**, silently. That is one of the
         two reasons a spatial ghost is a shadow object rather than an appended
         suffix; see @ref mftl_mod_communication.

---

## Boundary is a property of the surface, not of the partition {#topo_boundary}

Two different statements, and only one is partition-independent:

| Statement | Meaning | Changes with the partition? |
|---|---|---|
| `twin_idx == MFTL_ERROR` | no partner in **this** partition | **yes** — true at an open surface's rim *and* at a ghost halo's outer edge |
| `flags & MFTL_FLAG_BOUNDARY` | no partner anywhere in the mesh | no |

`MFTLMarkMeshBoundary` writes the flag **once, while the mesh is still complete**
— at generation time — onto every half-edge with no partner and onto both
endpoints of every such half-edge. From then on it travels with the entity
through redistribution and through remeshing.

Re-deriving it from `twin_idx` after a redistribution would promote every halo
rim into a fake boundary, which the remesher would then refuse to touch.

On a closed mesh the flag is clear everywhere, so code written against it behaves
identically on closed and open surfaces.

## Walking a fan {#topo_fan}

The one-ring of a vertex is generated by alternating the two involutions. From an
outgoing half-edge $h$, the next outgoing half-edge around the same origin is

$$
  h' = \mathrm{next}(\mathrm{twin}(h))
$$

and the clockwise step, used to rewind an open fan to its start, is its inverse

$$
  h'' = \mathrm{twin}(\mathrm{prev}(h))
$$

On an interior vertex this closes into a cycle; on a boundary vertex it
terminates at `MFTL_ERROR`.

### A boundary vertex has one MORE neighbour than incident triangles

The closing edge of an open fan is reachable only as an **incoming** half-edge —
its outgoing direction is the one that does not exist. So:

| Walker | Boundary vertex result |
|---|---|
| `MFTLGetVertexOneRing` (triangles) | correct: one per incident triangle |
| `MFTLGetVertexOneRingVertices` | correct: emits the extra neighbour **first**, keeping rotational order |
| `MFTLGetVertexOneRingHalfedges` | **short by one**, deliberately — its contract is *outgoing* half-edges |

Anything iterating **edges** (edge lengths, umbrella smoothing, remeshing
candidates) must use the vertex walker, or reach the missing edge as
`MFTLHePrevIdx(first)`. `src/remeshing/remesh_smooth.c` does exactly that, and
`test_one_ring_at_a_boundary_vertex_has_one_extra_neighbour` guards it.

### Every walk is bounded

`MFTL_MAX_VALENCE` bounds each loop, so a corrupt non-manifold fan asserts in
Debug instead of spinning forever inside an inline function with no diagnostics.

## Integrity checking {#topo_integrity}

`MFTLCheckMeshIntegrity` validates:

- twin symmetry — `twin(twin(h)) == h`
- `out_he_idx` really is an outgoing half-edge of its vertex
- every index inside its pool's active range
- the vertex a half-edge targets is alive

It does **not** check the implicit-indexing invariant itself. Nothing at the type
level stops a future routine from creating half-edges out of triple alignment,
and that would corrupt every traversal silently. `inv_implicit_indexing()` in
`tests/test_mesh_invariants.h` catches it in the test suite; asserting it inside
`MFTLObjectSetCounts` is recorded as deferred issue 2 in `CLAUDE.md`.

## The flag word {#topo_flags}

Every bit of `flags` is allocated in `mftl_topology.h`, including bits only
`src/remeshing/` reads. A bit registry split across headers is a bit registry
with a collision in its future, and layer 2 defining a constant that only layer 3
uses introduces no dependency.

| Bit | Owner | Survives a remesh? |
|---|---|---|
| #MFTL_FLAG_BOUNDARY | `src/topology` | **persistent** — a property of the surface |
| #MFTL_FLAG_DEAD | `src/remeshing` | transient — cleared by compaction, the only thing that observes it |
| #MFTL_FLAG_VISITED | `src/remeshing` | transient — cleared between sweeps |
| #MFTL_FLAG_FEATURE | `src/remeshing` | **persistent** — re-derived per pass, but travels with the entity in between (a split must hand it to both halves, or a ridge develops a gap) |
| #MFTL_FLAG_CORNER | `src/remeshing` | persistent, vertices only |
| #MFTL_FLAG_FROZEN | `src/remeshing` | transient — one parallel phase. **Recomputed from the partition after every migration, never transmitted**: it is a statement about *this* rank's halo, so a received copy would be another rank's answer to a different question |
